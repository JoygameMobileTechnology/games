# Ink-theme binder lore

Research checked 2026-09-25 for `src/tile-lore-ink.js`.

## Editorial boundaries

These are modern game designs inspired by Ming porcelain, Guo Xi and Xia Gui, not reproductions attributed to those artists or periods. The prose connects their named subjects to documented cultural associations or useful historical context. Later objects can document a continuing association without dating the game design to that object.

Sentences beginning **“Here,”** are curatorial interpretations of this game's motif within its theme. They do not assert that a culture traditionally assigned that exact meaning to the object. In particular, no symbolism is invented for one through six: the amounts and tiers identify the game faces. The six bamboo-tray interpretations are about household craft, not a historical six-stage ritual. Peony stages after/before the full bloom use interpretive readings of the documented prosperity association.

Existing aliases map the same subject between Eastern and Western editions. Source mappings below use the canonical subject keys; the named alias therefore inherits the same support.

## Interpretations with no asserted traditional emblem

These notes are marked “Here,” in the visible sentence. Their point is to offer a meaningful reading of the tile within its theme without pretending there is a verified traditional meaning for every object.

- **Ming porcelain:** pear, coral, hanging tag, temple bell, cloud scroll, pair of scrolls, lantern, incense burner, waterwheel, cherries, comb, spinning top, resting doe, dragonfly, closed parasol; peony tiers 1–3 and 6 extend a sourced floral association.
- **Guo Xi:** bridge, cloud, waterfall, reeds, gate, dragonfly, teapot, lute, panpipes, lantern, bell, moon, waterwheel, well, umbrella, sandal, incense burner, water buffalo, mortar, sailboat, flying heron, pagoda garden, crescent, bare tree, willow, cypress, ginkgo, stairs, river, tea bowl, creel, robe, ladder; pagoda tiers 2–6 apply contextual readings to architecture.
- **Xia Gui:** lotus leaf, swallow, dragonfly, basket, rain cape, conical hat, bound scroll, bell, oil lamp, bridge, frog, water buffalo, net, sandal, umbrella, mortar, snail, cloud, rooster, mushrooms, scale, sedan chair, perched swallow, crescent, ripples, creel, handcart, lantern, crab, well, ladle; all bamboo tray tiers.

The useful roles of ordinary objects (carrying, shelter, grinding, drawing water and transport) are explanatory context, not claims about supernatural power or a specific historical ritual. No unverified health effects or literal immortality claims are presented as fact.
