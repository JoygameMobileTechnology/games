# Tile lore: Dancheong, Dunhuang and Stained Glass

Researched 25 September 2026 for `src/tile-lore-sacred.js`.

These are short contextual notes for newly created game art, not identifications of museum objects. “Eastern” and “Western” are game editions; the same subject keeps its cultural context across editions. General Buddhist meanings are identified as such rather than attributed to every period or cave at Dunhuang. No numbered tile rank is presented as traditional numerology.

Entries introduced with **“Here”**, and the additional interpretive entries listed below, are authored readings of this collection. They are not claims that a culture assigned that exact meaning to the motif. This lets practical objects remain interesting without inventing a historical legend.

### Authored interpretation, not fixed traditional symbolism

**Fish, Open fan, Butterfly, Ginkgo leaf, Pomegranate, Temple gate, Barrel drum, Roof finial, Vase, Teapot, Hanging scroll, Woven basket, Kite, Lute, Guardian mask, Embroidered shoe, Parasol, Waterwheel, Acorn, Wooden comb, Key, Willow branch, Brush and inkstone, Ceremonial palanquin, Horse saddle, Rice sheaf** use a clearly signaled reading rooted in the motif’s role or this collection’s temple/craft setting. For example, the acorn’s potential and the rice sheaf’s shared labor are authorial readings, not alleged Korean religious codes. The **Pomegranate** note deliberately avoids claiming that this particular image documents a fertility custom.

**Blessing hand, Crown, Sheng mouth organ, Incense burner, Elephant, Peacock, Lion, Water jar, Oil lamp, Treasure chest, Bell, Caravan saddle, Sun medallion, Pomegranate branch, Butterfly, Balance scale, Travel tent, Crane, Oasis palm, Pilgrim staff, Desert fox, Scorpion** explicitly evoke a possible reading in this artwork. The hand is not identified as a specific mudra from an insufficiently precise drawing. The palm is a fictional oasis emblem, not a botanical claim about historic Dunhuang. The lion and elephant are not assigned an unverified named Buddhist role. The desert animals receive no invented protective or religious status.

### Authored interpretation, not a universal medieval code

**Open book, Oak leaf, Acorn, Ivy leaf, Thistle, Hourglass, Crown, Lyre, Swan, Stag, Butterfly, Dragonfly, Pear, Tree, Bell, Lantern, Sailing ship, Sun, Crescent moon, Comet, Feather, Horn, Balance scales, Bee and honeycomb, Daffodil, Rose sprig, Snail**, and **amber-star counts 1–6**, use explicitly signaled readings for this collection. These do not assert that every medieval window used the object in that way. In particular, no numbered group of stars is assigned a supposed doctrinal meaning, and the snail is not tied to a speculative medieval “knight versus snail” explanation.
